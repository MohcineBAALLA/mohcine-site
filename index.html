Professional Overview

Mohcine Baalla is a doctoral researcher in Computer Science at the Smart Systems Laboratory (SSL), National School of Computer Science and Systems Analysis (ENSIAS), Mohammed V University in Rabat, Morocco, under the supervision of Prof. Driss Bouzidi.

His doctoral investigation operates at the crossroads of Cybersecurity, Distributed Trust Management, and Social Graph Analytics. Mohcine's research formalizes novel relationship-layer adversarial threats in the Social Internet of Things (SIoT)—notably introducing the Fake Relationship Attack (FRA)—and engineers edge-native mitigation frameworks, continuous-time relational attention models, and privacy-preserving zero-knowledge verification protocols tailored for resource-constrained edge ecosystems and vehicular ad-hoc networks.

He is a recipient of the prestigious PhD-Associate Scholarship (PASS), an excellence-based national program awarded by the CNRST and the Ministry of Higher Education to support high-potential researchers in advanced scientific fields.

Research Focus & Methodology

Under the supervision of Prof. Driss Bouzidi at ENSIAS, my doctoral work addresses fundamental security vulnerabilities in decentralized Trust Management Systems across the Social Internet of Things (SIoT) and Vehicular Social Networks (SIoV).

Moving beyond conventional device-centric security, my research centers on developing algorithmic defenses against structural recommendation collusion (Fake Relationship Attacks), dynamic behavioral switching (Trust Manipulation Attacks), and malicious temporal mimicry.

The methodology bridges empirical IoT sensor simulations (Contiki-NG/Cooja), realistic vehicular mobility modeling (OMNeT++/Veins/SUMO), Dynamic Graph Neural Networks (PyG), and Zero-Knowledge Cryptography (zk-SNARKs) for verifiable, privacy-preserving trust.
