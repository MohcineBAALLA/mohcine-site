Professional Overview (Third-Person)

Mohcine Baalla is a doctoral researcher in Computer Science at the Smart Systems Laboratory (SSL), National School of Computer Science and Systems Analysis (ENSIAS), Mohammed V University in Rabat, Morocco, under the supervision of Prof. Driss Bouzidi.

His doctoral investigation operates at the crossroads of Cybersecurity, Distributed Trust Management, and Social Graph Analytics. Mohcine's research formalizes novel relationship-layer adversarial threats in the Social Internet of Things (SIoT)—notably introducing the Fake Relationship Attack (FRA)—and engineers edge-native mitigation frameworks, continuous-time relational attention models, and privacy-preserving zero-knowledge verification protocols tailored for resource-constrained edge ecosystems and vehicular ad-hoc networks.

He is a recipient of the prestigious PhD-Associate Scholarship (PASS), an excellence-based national program awarded by the CNRST and the Ministry of Higher Education to support high-potential researchers engaged in advanced strategic scientific domains.

Personal Statement & Methodology

"I am directed by Prof. Driss Bouzidi at ENSIAS Rabat. My research addresses fundamental security vulnerabilities in decentralized Trust Management Systems across the Social Internet of Things (SIoT) and Vehicular Social Networks (SIoV)."

"By moving beyond conventional device-centric security, I develop algorithmic defenses against structural recommendation collusion (Fake Relationship Attacks), dynamic behavioral switching (Trust Manipulation Attacks), and generative temporal mimicry."

"My methodologies combine cycle-accurate IoT sensor simulation (Contiki-NG/Cooja), realistic vehicular mobility modeling (OMNeT++/Veins/SUMO), Continuous-Time Dynamic Graph Neural Networks (PyG Temporal), and Zero-Knowledge Proofs (zk-SNARKs Groth16 / BN254)."

Pioneering Algorithmic Defenses Against Relational Collusion in SIoT

In multi-hop recommendation networks, colluding adversaries craft synthetic social ties to artificially inflate trust ratings. Our Semantic-Gated Structural Diversity Penalty (SDP) operates with edge-native O(k²) complexity to detect echo chambers and isolate hostile Defectors with 98.6% F1 accuracy without requiring global graph traversal.
